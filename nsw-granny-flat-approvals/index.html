NSW granny flat approvals in Port Macquarie

In short: In NSW, a granny flat (secondary dwelling) of up to 60 m² on a lot of at least 450 m² can often be approved quickly as complying development if it meets the Housing SEPP standards. Otherwise you lodge a development application with council.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal or planning advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises NSW Government and council information so you know what to expect. It is not legal or planning advice. Your builder, a certifier or council can confirm what applies to your lot.

What counts as a granny flat in NSW

The NSW Government calls a granny flat a secondary dwelling: a self-contained dwelling within, attached to, or separate from the main house on the same lot. Secondary dwellings are permitted in residential zones R1 to R5, and in other zones if the council's local environmental plan allows them. You can only have one principal and one secondary dwelling on the lot, and the lot can't be subdivided.

Source: NSW Planning, Secondary dwellings.

Complying development (CDC)

The planning rules for secondary dwellings are in the State Environmental Planning Policy (Housing) 2021. A granny flat can be approved as complying development, usually through a private certifier, if it meets all the standards in Schedule 1 of the Housing SEPP. Two key ones are a lot area of at least 450 m² and a maximum floor area of 60 m² for the granny flat. The standards also cover things like height, setbacks and site coverage. If your lot is under 450 m², a CDC isn't available unless the granny flat is entirely inside the existing house.

The Housing SEPP doesn't require extra car parking for a secondary dwelling, but check any existing consent before removing parking. A new main house and granny flat can be approved together under one CDC if both meet the relevant codes.

Development application (DA)

If the design doesn't meet every complying development standard, you can still lodge a development application with council, which is assessed against the council's own controls.

Local council: Port Macquarie

Your local council is Port Macquarie-Hastings Council. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a granny flat. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Parts of Port Macquarie-Hastings are covered by flood, coastal or bush fire mapping. Check the council planning certificate for your address, because these overlays can change floor levels, setbacks and what can be built.

Licences, contracts and insurance

In NSW, a contractor needs a licence for residential building or trade work valued at more than $5,000 (labour and materials, including GST), and there must be a written contract above that amount. Jobs from $5,000 to $20,000 use a small-jobs contract, and jobs over $20,000 a large-jobs contract. For work over $20,000, the contractor must take out Home Building Compensation cover and give you the certificate before taking a deposit or starting work. You can check a licence on the NSW Government licence check.

Sources: NSW Government, General building work; NSW Government, Insurance cover for building work; NSW Government, Contracts for residential building work.
